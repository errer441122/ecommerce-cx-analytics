Reviews and surveysStars, comments, NPS.They reach whoever answers, often not the person who opened the box.

The full analysisThe brief
  • Statistical tests, NPS proxy, logistic regressionAll in the full analysis. Olist has no real NPS question, so the stars stand in for it.
  • Why it was not enoughIt describes a shop where people leave stars. In B2B there are none, and a helpdesk already sorts the complaints it receives.
  • What came nextThe same question, asked where B2B customers do answer: the credit notes. My own analysis of the wholesaler had dropped them in the first cleaning step.

What it cannot do

  • Say why the goods came back.If the export has a reason column it shows the most frequent one. Otherwise the list says where to look, and a person has to ask.
  • Judge items that sell little.Below 20 customers in 60 days the answer is "too few to say". The thresholds can be lowered, at the price of more false alarms.
  • Read every company's credit notes the same way.In the public data an invoice starting with C is a cancellation; the ones dated after the purchase are read as goods sent back. Rebates and price corrections without a quantity are left out.
  • Prove it on a company's own catalogue, yet.It has been tested on public data and on files written by hand. Leaving the items out of a real campaign is still to be tried.
